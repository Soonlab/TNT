### 3.4 Pre-CRT immune and TME landscape at single-signature resolution (Fig 4; Table 2, Table S3; Supp Fig S7)

Having established that pre-CRT eventual good responders are tumor-intrinsically proliferative and DNA-repair-proficient (§3.3), we next resolved the immune/TME compartment at single-signature resolution across the 33 pre-CRT RNA-seq samples (18 good, 15 bad).

TME signature overview (Fig 4A).
A radar plot of the eight top-level TME axes — CD8 cytotoxic, T-cell infiltration, B-cell infiltration, Treg, MHC-I, MHC-II, TLS, and tumor cell-cycle — summarises the pre-CRT composition by response.
Good responders showed a higher tumor cell-cycle axis (consistent with §3.3) together with a modest CD8-cytotoxic skew; MHC-II was the only axis trending lower in good responders.
No individual radar axis reached P < 0.05 at the single-axis level; the CD8-cytotoxic signal is fully established only in external meta-analysis (§3.11, Fig 7).

Twenty-two-signature heatmap (Fig 4B).
Per-sample z-scores of 22 curated immune/TME signatures (cytolytic, activation, proliferation, exhaustion, antigen presentation, chemokine, Treg, B-cell, TLS, stromal, EMT; definitions in Methods) are displayed as an unsupervised column-clustered heatmap annotated by response.
Samples did not separate cleanly by response label — consistent with pre-CRT immune state being a modest rather than dominant axis — but a good-enriched cluster was apparent on the cell-cycle / CD8-proliferation side, and a bad-enriched cluster on the MHC-II / stromal side.

DEG volcano (Fig 4C).
DESeq2 of pre-CRT good vs bad, adjusted for sex and clinical T stage, yielded no single gene at FDR < 0.1 (minimum BH-adjusted P = 0.496).
The absence of single-gene significance, combined with strong GSEA signal (§3.3), indicates that the predictive structure in this cohort lives at the pathway/signature level rather than in individual transcripts — a pattern consistent with the modest nested-CV AUC reported in §3.5.

Signature-response forest (Fig 4D).
To summarise effect sizes of the 22 signatures in a single display, we plotted each signature as a lollipop of the good-vs-bad standardised mean difference with its Mann–Whitney P.
The three nominally significant pre-CRT associations were CD8 proliferation (P = 0.035, ↑ good), E2F targets (P = 0.035, ↑ good), and MHC-II (P = 0.074, ↓ good).
We note explicitly that the original "CD8 proliferation" signature as commonly defined overlaps heavily with tumor-intrinsic cell-cycle genes (MKI67, TOP2A, MCM2–7, CCNB1/2, CDK1, PCNA); the external-validation analysis (§3.11) therefore uses a purified CD8-cytotoxic signature (GZMA/B/H/K, PRF1, IFNG, CD8A/B, NKG7, GNLY, CXCL9/10/11, TBX21, EOMES) separated from a tumor cell-cycle signature.

CD8 effector × exhaustion biaxial (Fig 4E).
Plotting each pre-CRT sample in the CD8-effector (GZMA/B, PRF1, IFNG, CD8A/B) vs CD8-exhaustion (PDCD1, LAG3, TIGIT, HAVCR2, CTLA4) plane, bad responders occupied a broader exhaustion-elevated / effector-low quadrant, whereas good responders clustered closer to the effector-high / exhaustion-moderate diagonal.
The biaxial view is hypothesis-generating (per-axis P > 0.05 in 33 samples); it motivates the post-CRT reprogramming analysis (§3.8, Fig 6), where within-good CD8 exhaustion rises in parallel with MHC-II and Treg — the inflamed-TIL post-treatment state.

Tertiary lymphoid structure score (Fig 4F).
TLS was scored by the 9-gene Cabrita signature (CCL2, CCL3, CCL4, CCL5, CCL8, CCL18, CCL19, CCL21, CXCL13) [31] at baseline.
Pre-CRT TLS scores trended higher in eventual good responders but did not reach significance at n = 33 (P = 0.19).
Post-CRT TLS dynamics, together with IGH clonotype expansion from TRUST4, are reported in §3.8.

Summary.
Pre-CRT immune axes show a direction-of-effect consistent with the final cascade model but individually under-powered at n = 33; the strongest baseline signal is not immune but tumor-intrinsic pathway activity (§3.3), and the immune axis becomes the dominant, externally reproducible signal only when (i) the radiation-induced cascade is read paired (§3.8, Fig 6) and (ii) when examined across > 1,000 external nCRT patients with a purified CD8-cytotoxic signature (§3.11, Fig 7).
