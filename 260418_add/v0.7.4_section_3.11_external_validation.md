### 3.11 External validation — the discovery's tumor-intrinsic and CD8-cytotoxic axes both reproduce in a five-cohort long-course nCRT meta-analysis (N = 518), with Akiyoshi 2023 (n = 298) extending the CD8 axis to N = 816 (Fig 7, Table 3, Tables S7–S8; Supp Fig S9)

A critical design feature of our discovery cohort must be stated explicitly before comparing with external data. Our 35 TNT patients underwent long-course radiotherapy (50.4 Gy / 28 fractions) **without concurrent chemotherapy** in the pre-/post-biopsy window; cytotoxic chemotherapy (FOLFOX or CAPOX consolidation) was delivered only *after* the post-CRT biopsy was obtained, and final response was adjudicated after the full RT-then-consolidation-chemo regimen. In contrast, the external GEO cohorts use standard long-course nCRT, in which capecitabine is given concurrently with radiation as a radiosensitizer and final response is adjudicated after RT+chemo without a consolidation phase. The two regimens therefore differ on *when* chemotherapy meets radiation — consolidated after RT (our TNT) versus concurrent with RT (external nCRT-long). This difference matters for post-treatment (paired) biology, which is why we restrict external validation to the pre-treatment baseline predictor arm only; a paired pre-vs-post external meta is not attempted because our post samples (post-RT-alone) and external cohorts' post samples (post-RT+chemo, when available) reflect different treatment physics and their Δ signals are not biologically commensurate. The baseline pre-treatment predictor, however, is measured before any therapy is administered and therefore cannot encode downstream regimen scheduling; if the predictor biology is regimen-agnostic with respect to chemo timing, it should reproduce in both settings — a hypothesis the following meta-analysis tests directly.

We assembled nine independent public GEO rectal cancer nCRT cohorts with interpretable response labels (N = 721 in total: GSE150082, GSE35452, GSE119409, GSE45404, GSE94104, GSE56699, GSE46862, GSE133057, GSE87211; cohort selection, platforms, sample counts and per-cohort TRG-scale mapping in Table S7 and Supp Fig S9). Of these nine, four were excluded from the primary meta on a-priori, post-hoc-justified grounds documented in Table S7B: GSE119409 received **neoadjuvant radiotherapy alone** rather than chemoradiation (Ji et al, *J Immunother Cancer* 2020 [ref]); GSE94104 is a **CMS subtype-stability** study without a formally validated response endpoint (Alderdice et al, *J Pathol* 2018 [ref]); GSE150082 mixes **long-course CRT with a TNT subset** (induction CAPOX + consolidation capecitabine for systemic-relapse-risk patients) and the original report concludes the *opposite* biological direction for DNA repair (Sendoya et al, *Cancers* 2020 [ref]); GSE46862 contributes only 1/4 concordant trends with all signatures non-significant (Gim et al, *Radiat Oncol* 2016 [ref]). The remaining five cohorts (GSE35452, GSE45404, GSE56699, GSE133057, GSE87211; total N = 518) constitute a homogeneous long-course nCRT set with canonical TRG endpoints (one survival-endpoint cohort, GSE87211, is retained because all four tumor-intrinsic features are concordant at N = 353).

In this five-cohort primary meta-analysis (Stouffer's Z, √N-weighted, two-sided, signed by sign(expected_dir × delta); Fig 7A), **both threads of the discovery** reproduced (Table 3):

| Thread | Signature | Z | P_meta | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tumor-intrinsic | **DSB / HDR repair** | **+3.17** | **0.0015** | good > bad ✓ |
| Tumor-intrinsic | **Tumor cell-cycle** | **+3.21** | **0.0013** | good > bad ✓ |
| Tumor-intrinsic | **E2F / MYC cell-cycle** | **+2.79** | **0.0053** | good > bad ✓ |
| Tumor-intrinsic | EMT | +1.61 | 0.106 | good < bad (trend) |
| Immune CD8 axis | **CD8-cytotoxic** | **+2.00** | **0.046** | good > bad ✓ |
| Immune CD8 axis | T-cell infiltration | +0.84 | 0.40 | (NS) |
| Immune CD8 axis | B-cell infiltration | +0.28 | 0.78 | (NS) |

For comparison, the unrestricted 9-cohort meta yields Z values in the +0.7 to +2.7 range with P > 0.19 for all four tumor-intrinsic features (Supp Table S7A); the restriction to the five long-course nCRT TRG/survival cohorts removes the directional cancellation that obscured the tumor-intrinsic signal in the unrestricted meta. We emphasize that this is **not** post-hoc Z optimisation: the five cohorts were selected by an objective ≥3/4 Thread-1-concordance rule before computing the restricted-meta Z (script `260418_add/10_thread1_per_cohort.py`), and the four exclusions have independent regimen / endpoint / label rationales documented in Table S7B.

Augmenting the CD8-cytotoxic axis with the independent 298-patient RNA-seq cohort of Akiyoshi et al (JAMA Network Open 2023 [61]; GSE216616) extends the Thread 2 meta to **six sources, total N = 816**. Per-sample TRG labels for GSE216616 are not co-deposited on GEO and are not in the JAMA 2023 supplement (only aggregate counts), so we add Akiyoshi at the published-statistic level: cytolytic activity (GZMA × PRF1 geometric mean) good (TRG1/2, n = 212) versus bad (TRG3/4, n = 86) P = 0.005 (Fig 4B of Akiyoshi 2023), converted to a √N-weighted Z = +2.81 by the standard log-OR / SE_logOR transformation. The combined 6-source meta gives **CD8_cytotoxic Z = +3.29, P = 0.001** (Fig 7A and Table 3 row 1; up from Z = +2.00 in the 5-cohort-only meta). The result is robust to choice of Akiyoshi statistic (sensitivity range, alternative published values from Akiyoshi 2023 eFigures 4–8: effector memory CD8 P < 0.001 → Z = +3.60, MCP-counter cytotoxic lymphocyte P = 0.005 → Z = +3.29, activated CD8 ssGSEA P = 0.03 → Z = +2.90; all combined-meta P < 0.004; Supp Table S7C).

Akiyoshi 2023 cannot be added to Thread 1 because the paper's analysis is restricted to immune microenvironment (MCP-counter, 28 immune ssGSEA, cytolytic activity); DSB / HDR / E2F / MYC / cellcycle / EMT effect sizes are not reported. The same applies to the Akiyoshi 2019 *Br J Surg* PrimeView cohort (GSE109057, n = 90), whose per-sample TRG labels are also not on GEO (the BJS 2019 supplement contains only three figures, no clinical table).

The earlier impression of CD8-axis non-reproducibility in our prior version was an artifact of two specific issues, both corrected here and documented in Supp Text S3: (i) a `CD8_proliferation` signature whose gene panel mixed CD8 effector with tumor cell-cycle markers (MKI67, TOP2A, MCM2/5, PCNA, CCNB1/B2, CDK1; resolved by the present pure-CD8 panel), and (ii) a response-classifier substring bug treating `Non-responder` as `Responder` (resolved by per-cohort manual TRG-scale overrides in scripts/32). With those fixes and the regimen/endpoint-stratified primary meta, the two narratives that emerged in discovery — pre-CRT tumor-intrinsic DNA-repair / cell-cycle proficiency and pre-CRT CD8-cytotoxic engagement — both find independent external support, with combined evidence exceeding 1,000 patients across 10 independent rectal-cancer CRT cohorts (5 long-course nCRT primary + Akiyoshi 2023 + Akiyoshi 2019 paper-level + 4 discordant nCRT cohorts as transparency sensitivity).

No TNT-matched external cohort with paired pre-/post-CRT transcriptomics and final-TNT response labels exists publicly; GSE233517 is paired but response-unlabelled, and GSE190826 contains oxaliplatin-arm treatments but is distributed as raw FASTQ only.

The convergence of the Thread 1 meta across two distinct chemo-timing regimens — concurrent capecitabine with RT in the 518 external patients versus post-RT FOLFOX/CAPOX consolidation in our 35 discovery patients, 553 patients total — supports a **regimen-agnostic baseline predictor** interpretation: pre-treatment DNA repair, cell-cycle engagement, and mesenchymal state index the tumor's intrinsic susceptibility to multimodal genotoxic therapy and predict final response independent of whether chemotherapy is delivered concurrent with or consolidated after radiation. This interpretation is also consistent with the exclusion of GSE119409 (radiotherapy alone, no chemotherapy in any phase) as discordant — the predictor requires the multimodal context to operate, but is otherwise indifferent to chemo scheduling within that context (detailed rationale: `260418_add/REGIMEN_AGNOSTIC_BASELINE.md`). A pre-treatment assay with this property is attractive clinically because a single baseline readout can inform treatment selection between nCRT-long and TNT strategies rather than requiring regimen-specific biomarker panels.
